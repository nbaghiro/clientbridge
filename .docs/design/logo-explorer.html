Clientbridge Logo exploration

Six logomark directions on the bridge idea — Clientbridge connects a provider to their clients. Each is shown as the bare mark, the horizontal and stacked lockups, monochrome, reversed on dark, an iOS-style app icon, and favicon sizes (16 / 24 / 32). All are single-path SVG, so they recolor with the theme and stay crisp at any size. Pick a direction; I'll refine.

Arch classic overpass — sturdy, civic, recognizable

A single rounded arch on a deck. The most literal “bridge”; reads instantly at small sizes and pairs well with a serious, trustworthy tone.

Span suspension — modern, engineered, premium

Cable, deck, and hangers. More detailed and contemporary; signals scale and reliability. Watch it at favicon size — the hangers can thin out.

Arches viaduct — rhythm, continuity, “many clients”

Repeating arches suggest an ongoing book of business. Distinctive silhouette, slightly more decorative.

Link connection — two endpoints, one arc

Two nodes (you ↔ client) joined by a span. Leans into the relationship/CRM meaning over the literal structure; friendliest and most abstract.

Rise soft curve — calm, approachable (current direction)

A gentle bridge curve over a baseline — close to the mark used in the theme explorer. Warm and unintimidating; great for a solo-provider audience.

Beam minimal deck + piers — structural, neutral

The most reductive: a deck on two piers. Almost a “table”; very clean, scales beautifully, but the least overtly “bridge”.

Monogram letterform — the Clientbridge “C” as a bold arc

An open C that doubles as an arch. Works hard as a standalone avatar / app glyph; less obviously a “bridge”.

Cable-stayed single mast, fanned cables — engineered

A modern cable-stayed bridge: one tower, radiating stays. Crisper than the suspension Span and a bit more iconic.

Chevron A-frame peak — decisive, structural

An angular peak over a deck. Bold and confident; reads as “up and to the right” growth as much as a bridge.

Brackets two brackets converging — abstract connection

> < meeting in the middle. Developer-friendly and modern; the most abstract take on “bridging two sides”.

Keystone arch with its keystone called out — trust

The Arch with a solid keystone at the crown — a focal mark plus a subtle “the piece that holds it together” story.

Fold folded / zigzag span — geometric, energetic

A faceted, origami-like span. Distinctive and lively; a touch more playful and less literal.

Wave a node radiating arcs — network / signal

One point sending out arcs — the “stay connected” relationship reading. Feels closer to messaging / CRM than civil engineering.

Trio three endpoints bridged — your whole book

Three nodes joined by spans — “you, connected to all your clients”. Friendly and scalable; verges on a generic network mark.

More in the Cable-stayed family — engineered, structural directions with masts, stays, and trusses:

Twin twin-tower cable-stayed — the iconic modern bridge

Two masts with stays fanning from each. The most recognizably “big bridge”; richest detail, so mind the favicon size.

Suspension twin towers + a draped main cable — Golden-Gate energy

Two towers and a single draped cable over the deck. Classic and premium; the cable’s sweep is the signature.

Tied arch bowstring — an arch tied to its deck with hangers

An arch held down by vertical hangers (the Span, inverted into an arch). Elegant and unmistakably structural.

A-Pylon an A-frame tower with stays — architectural

A single A-shaped pylon with stays fanning to the deck. Bold and modern; a stronger standalone glyph than the single mast.

Truss a Warren-truss girder — industrial, precise

Top and bottom chords with a zigzag web. The most “engineering” of the set; geometric and distinctive, less soft.

Harp harp-pattern stays from one mast — orderly, refined

A variation on Cable-stayed with parallel-leaning stays (harp vs fan). Calmer and more rhythmic than the fanned version.
